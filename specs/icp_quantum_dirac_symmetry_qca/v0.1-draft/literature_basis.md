## Active-law basis

Alessandro Bisio, Giacomo Mauro D'Ariano, and Alessandro Tosini, “Quantum Field
as a quantum cellular automaton: the Dirac free evolution in one dimension,”
*Annals of Physics* 354 (2015) 244–264,
<https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.2839>.

Design use: the direct two-channel law, its symmetry-derived status, its exact
dispersion, and the low-mass/low-momentum Dirac comparison.

Claim boundary: neither the law nor its dispersion, group velocity, general
continuum relation, or symmetry derivation is novel here.

Alessandro Bisio, Giacomo Mauro D'Ariano, and Alessandro Tosini,
“Dirac quantum cellular automaton in one dimension: Zitterbewegung and
scattering from potential,” <https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0461>.

Design use: direct-family Zitterbewegung and scalar-potential scattering as
motivation for the frozen observable witnesses and for testing a separately
charged leading potential phase.

Claim boundary: the present potential prefix, ordering, finite-domain maps,
thresholds, and leakage gates are protocol choices; the cited scattering and
Zitterbewegung phenomena are not novel claims.

## Continuum target and encoding context

Pablo Arrighi, Stefano Facchini Forets, and Vincent Nesme, “The Dirac equation
as a quantum walk: higher dimensions, observational convergence,”
<https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.3524>.

Design use: the finite low-momentum encoding, continuum target comparison,
and the distinction between state-vector error and operational discrepancy.

Claim boundary: this source controls neither the active direct finite-lattice
law nor its hard-core lift. It defined the verified parent product-walk family,
which remains an excluded substitution here.

## Global-spectrum failure boundary

Chaitanya Gupta and Anthony J. Short, “Fermion Doubling in Dirac Quantum
Walks,” accepted by *Physical Review A* on `2026-07-01`,
<https://doi.org/10.1103/kkz3-fthm> and
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15885>.

Design use: the distinction between low-energy doublers and high-energy
Dirac-like pseudo-doublers; the implication of modular quasienergy for
second-quantized and interacting models; and a separate alternative walk
family that avoids these modes.

Claim boundary: the active law has an exact pi-mode pseudodoubler and is not
globally doubler-free.  The alternative walk family is not an allowed
post-outcome repair of this search.

Chaitanya Gupta and Anthony J. Short, “The Dirac Vacuum in Discrete
Spacetime,” *Quantum* 9 (2025) 1845,
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03466>.

Design use: the additional positive/negative-energy boundary at modular
quasienergy `+/-pi/delta` and its interacting-vacuum risk.

## Locality and index basis

David Gross, Vincent Nesme, Holger Vogts, and Reinhard F. Werner, “Index theory
of one dimensional quantum walks and cellular automata,” *Communications in
Mathematical Physics* 310 (2012) 419–454,
<https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.3675>.

Design use: classification of locally implementable one-dimensional walks and
QCAs.  The active two-layer partition is a specific trivial-index realization,
not a new general decomposition theorem.

## Fermionic-lift basis

Terence C. Farrelly and Anthony J. Short, “Causal Fermions in Discrete
Spacetime,” *Physical Review A* 89 (2014) 012302,
<https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.4652>.

Leonard Mlodinow and Todd A. Brun, “Quantum field theory from a quantum
cellular automaton in one spatial dimension and a no-go theorem in higher
dimensions,” *Physical Review A* 102 (2020) 042211,
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08927>.

Design use: causal fermionic encoding and a multiparticle QCA that matches a
walk in the one-particle sector and approaches free fermionic field theory.

Claim boundary: the present ordinary qubit SWAP and edge-scatter gates fix
double occupation with phase `+1`; the canonical fermionic lifts use the
one-particle determinant `-1`.  Matching the one-excitation law does not make
the active hard-core extension a fermionic Fock lift or establish a
multiparticle field theory.
