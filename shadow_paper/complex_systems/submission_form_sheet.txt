Paper Title:
The Shadow Knows: Interaction Records That Outlive Their Carriers in Reversible Second-Order Cellular Automata

Abstract:
In a reversible dynamics no information is destroyed, so the record of an interaction must persist somewhere. Where it goes is determined exactly in the 256 second-order extensions of the elementary cellular automata. Bounded, autonomous, slower-than-light coherences are first identified by frozen, outcome-blind searches and then collided. For each collision an exact ledger separates the information carried away by the outgoing coherences from the information left in the background. Every resolved coherence is then erased and a fresh probe is sent through the residue. Across 91 collision pairs in 25 rules, pairs whose background returns to vacuum give the probe no information, while in all 45 pairs that leave a residue the probe recovers all of the information held only by the background, and in 27 of them the probe coherence carries part of it away. All such shadows occur in rules with an oscillating vacuum.

Keywords:
reversible cellular automata; second-order cellular automata; information dynamics; gliders; information storage; causal intervention
