\begin{abstract}
Biological organization can persist while the material components that realize
it turn over. We tested whether a relational constraint and an expression
lineage can survive such replacement in a finite local stochastic lattice
model. Three strict events disconnected tracked regions after loss of an
original bridge and reconnected them through a causally necessary, disjoint
bridge under payload-free dynamics. Prospective ancestry showed lineage
survival after death of its initial address. In 192 fresh branches,
one-decision interventions reopened all 36 targeted successor routes; all six
gate interventions produced an immediate child, and 28 of 30 exhaustion
interventions eventually produced handoff. Vetoing 164 naturally realized
unique-child events exposed an alternate continuation in 122 branches and
lineage loss in 42. Sequentially vetoing the next realized child produced an
observed policy cut by depth eight in 143 branches; an independent cohort
reproduced cuts in 68 of 79 branches across four new source geometries. These
results show, within the frozen simulator, that continuation depends on both
the availability and timely realization of lawful successors and that
counterfactual redundancy can recur along changed histories. To distinguish
contraction from enabling, we define a signed support deformation,
$\chi(R_1\!:\!R_0)=\log[\mu(R_0)/\mu(R_1)]$. For nested uniform supports it
equals forward Kullback--Leibler divergence under pruning and negative reverse
Kullback--Leibler divergence under expansion. This formalizes a bounded
connection to the adjacent possible, but the experiments did not enumerate
complete reachable supports or establish open-ended evolution, substrate
independence, or physical correspondence.
\end{abstract}
