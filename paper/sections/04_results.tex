\subsection{Ordered terminal events and a causal continuation gate}

Finding 066 instrumented every selected hard-transfer candidate on the terminal
tick for 96 fresh branches. The observer passed exact state-and-RNG equivalence
and recorded 20,748 terminal candidate events. The ordered ledger classified all
96 outcomes without collisions: 57 death-tick child activations, 16
preexisting-descendant continuations, 19 source-exhaustion deaths, three
transfer-gate deaths, and one target-preemption death. In a preregistered replay
of one gate-failure branch, permitting exactly one failed successor gate while
still consuming its original random draw changed the outcome from lineage death
to one collision-free ancestry-preserving child. This supports a bounded causal
claim: continuation can depend on realizing an eligible successor before the
source expends its terminating amplitude.

A complete screen of \GCEightScreenCases{} continuations from 16 unused
initialization seeds found four singleton-eligible strict transports across two

target availability and was followed by handoff four offsets later. These
results separate two causal conditions: route availability and successor
realization. Reopening opportunity is causal, but it is not by itself sufficient
for continuation.
