## Why this does not answer the question
- In the silent rules every probe species moves at |v| = 1, the maximum speed for radius-1 rules (known: silent
  movers are light-speed only). Something at the light-cone edge cannot be delayed by what lies ahead of it, so its
  orbit and speed are unchanged (kappa = 1), and any front it leaves moves at 1. Universality is therefore forced by
  kinematics, not by any metric-like property of the record.
- The rules with slower (sub-light) travellers, 147 and 201, have only time-shifted-vacuum media, where kappa = 1 is
  exact by symmetry. No slow clock crossed a genuinely different medium, so the medium-vs-metric question for slow
  clocks is **not assessed at this depth**.
- Descriptive: most travellers do not stay themselves inside a record. They become fronts that rewrite it: 26sL,
  58sL, 82sR and 114sR 100%; 26sR and 82sL 69%. In 58sR and 114sL no orbit was found.

## What would answer it

## Lay version
We asked whether a footprint slows every clock that passes through it by the same amount (as gravity would) or each
differently (like glass bending colours differently). In these worlds every traveller that crossed a real footprint was
already moving at the top speed, and nothing can slow the very front of the light cone. So the answer came out "all
the same", but only for that boring reason. We need slower travellers before we learn anything. One real finding: inside
a footprint most travellers don't stay themselves. They turn into a wave that rewrites the footprint as it goes.
