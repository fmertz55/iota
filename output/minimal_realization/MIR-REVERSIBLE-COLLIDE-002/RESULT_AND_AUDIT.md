   L are mirror-speed objects), and every bounded outcome kept the same speed set. A total of 0 in and
   0 out is therefore conserved by symmetry alone. An informative test needs unequal-speed pairs.
2. The 20 transmutations are real changes of species at unchanged speeds: 2 in -> 2 out, each
   leaving with the same speed but a different internal pattern (rules 123, 147, 183, 201, 237).
   It is not yet checked whether the "new" species is another catalogued object (e.g. the mirror

3. Wall speeds: the tracker followed "the longest run", whose identity changes between the two
   sample times, giving impossible speeds > 1 (e.g. 5.8). H_B2 is void and must be re-measured.
4. Unresolved (28) come from rules 143, 151, 171, 213, 233, 241: outgoing material that is wide or not periodic.

## Lay version
When two of these information-objects collide head-on, they never vanish. Most often they bounce
or pass on unchanged (101 of 200). Sometimes both come out as different kinds of object travelling
at the same speeds (20), like particles changing identity in a collision. In some rules the
collision instead opens a growing bubble of "flipped" background, and that happens only where the
background itself flickers, as we predicted beforehand. We can't yet say whether anything like
momentum is conserved: our test pairs were mirror images, so the balance was automatic. That is
the next test.
