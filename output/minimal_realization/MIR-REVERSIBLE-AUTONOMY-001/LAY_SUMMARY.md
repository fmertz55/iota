# Can pure information rules make particle-like things? (lay summary, 2026-11-03)

Iota asks whether particle-like objects can arise from rules about information alone.
We tested the simplest information-preserving rules: each cell's next value depends on its
neighbours and on its own previous value, and no information is ever destroyed.
All 256 such rules were tested, with every test frozen before it was run.

What we found:
- **Started from a single event**, these rules mostly make expanding sprays or
  light-speed signals, not objects.
- **Started from a small 2–4 bit seed**, 13 rules make true objects. These are compact
  patterns that stay together, move at a steady speed *slower than light*
  (1/2 or about 1/3 of the maximum), and keep going when cut out of their surroundings
  and placed alone. 36 rule-and-seed combinations pass this test.
- **The surprise:** every one of these slow objects lives in an active vacuum, where the
  empty background itself flickers in a regular rhythm. In a truly empty, silent
  background, no slow object appeared.

Honest limits: moving patterns like these are known in this family of rules from
earlier work (Fredkin, Toffoli & Margolus). A single event never condensed into one by
itself. Whether these objects collide, scatter, or conserve anything like energy has not
been tested yet.
