# Do information-particles collide like particles? (lay summary, 2026-11-03)

In the last round we found rules that make small, steady, slower-than-light "objects".
We had noticed that every one of them lived in a flickering background, so we guessed that
slow objects need an active vacuum. A larger, fairer search proved that guess **wrong**:
many rules with a completely quiet background also host slow objects. We report that openly.

Next we aimed pairs of objects at each other (192 real collisions across 24 rules):
- **They never simply vanish.** That is guaranteed: these rules never destroy information,
  so two objects cannot turn into nothing. All 464 runs obeyed this.
- **Most often, two objects come out** (126 of 192), but changed by the encounter, for example
  shifted or swapped. That looks like a real interaction, not a ghostly pass-through.
- **Sometimes they pass straight through** each other untouched (24).
- **Sometimes the collision opens a growing bubble** in which the background flickers in the opposite
  rhythm (41). It is like a collision changing the vacuum itself.

Honest limits: our first automatic scorer had two bugs, which we found by looking at the pictures
(many "pass-throughs" never actually met). The numbers above are the corrected ones and are exploratory.
A properly frozen re-test comes next, including whether anything like momentum is conserved.
